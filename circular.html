The lens is a singular isothermal sphere. When the lens is circular, the number of images depends only on how far the background galaxy is from the centre. If the background galaxy lies inside the dashed circle in the small box (|β| < θE), the lens forms two images of it, on opposite sides of the lens galaxy. If it lies outside the circle, the lens forms one image. The Einstein radius is θE = 4π(σ/c)² DLS/DS, where σ is the velocity dispersion of the lens galaxy, DLS is the distance from the lens to the background galaxy and DS is the distance to the background galaxy, in a flat universe with H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc and Ωm = 0.3. An elliptical lens breaks this symmetry: see the elliptical lens. The equations are given in the model note.
